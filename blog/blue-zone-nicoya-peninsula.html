Living in a Blue Zone: What the Nicoya Peninsula Teaches About a Long, Healthy Life

Home  /  Blog  /  Living in a Blue Zone

The Nicoya Peninsula is one of only five places in the world known as a Blue Zone, regions where an unusual number of people live into their 90s and past 100, and stay active and independent along the way. For many of our buyers, that is part of the appeal: a home in a place where a simpler, healthier way of life is still part of everyday culture.

What is a Blue Zone?

The term was coined by demographers Michel Poulain and Giovanni Mario Pes together with National Geographic explorer and author Dan Buettner, who set out to find the longevity hot spots of the world. They identified five: Okinawa in Japan, Sardinia in Italy, Ikaria in Greece, Loma Linda in California and the Nicoya Peninsula in Costa Rica. People in these regions live significantly longer than the general population, with fewer cases of heart disease, diabetes and other chronic illness.

What the research found in Nicoya

Costa Rican demographer Luis Rosero Bixby of the University of Costa Rica, working with researchers from Stanford and the University of California, studied Nicoya using birth records from the civil registry, which has existed in Costa Rica since 1883. Their work showed that older adults in Nicoya had a death rate about 30% lower than the rest of the country, a remarkable result in a country that already has a high life expectancy. The advantage was strongest among men: a 60 year old Nicoyan man had about twice the chance of reaching 90 as a man of the same age in the United States.

The area that was studied covers five cantons in the Guanacaste half of the peninsula: Nicoya, Santa Cruz, Carrillo, Hojancha and most of Nandayure. In 2021 it was home to 57 centenarians and more than 1,000 people over 90.

The habits behind a long life

No single secret explains Nicoya. Researchers describe it as many small things that add up:

  • A plan de vida. A clear sense of purpose and a reason to get up every morning, whether it is family, work in the garden or the community.
  • Family close by. Elders usually live with or near their children and grandchildren, who look after them day to day.
  • Natural movement. A lifetime of physical work outdoors, farming, walking and carrying, instead of time at the gym.
  • Simple, plant rich food. Beans, corn tortillas, rice, squash and tropical fruit, eaten in modest portions, with a light meal at the end of the day.
  • Faith and friendship. Strong social ties and regular visits with neighbors lower stress and keep people connected.
  • Sunshine and fresh air. Time outside every day, in a warm climate all year.
  • Mineral rich water. Local water is naturally high in calcium. Many residents believe it helps, although this has not been proven.
  • Access to care. Costa Rica's public health system reaches even small rural communities.

An honest note

Lifestyles are changing here as they are everywhere. A 2023 study by Rosero Bixby found that the longevity advantage is strongest among Nicoyans born before 1930 and is fading in younger generations, as diets and daily routines become more modern. The lesson is that longevity comes from the way people live, not from the place itself. The good news is that this way of life is still easy to find, and to choose, on the peninsula.

Living the Blue Zone way on the southern peninsula

Our homes are on the southern tip of the Nicoya Peninsula, in Paquera, Tambor, Tango Mar and Cóbano. The official Blue Zone study area lies further north, but the same coastline, the same warm climate and the same unhurried, family centered culture are part of daily life here. It is easy to build Blue Zone habits into your routine:

  • Start the day with a walk or swim on the calm beaches of Bahía Ballena.
  • Shop for fresh fruit, vegetables and beans at local farmers markets and small family stores.
  • Plant a garden; mangoes, bananas, herbs and vegetables grow easily in this climate.
  • Hike to the Cocolito waterfall, explore Curú Wildlife Refuge or the Cabo Blanco reserve.
  • Get to know your neighbors, join community events and take time for long meals with friends.
